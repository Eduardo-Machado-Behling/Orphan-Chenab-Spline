The Orphan–⁠Chenab stream, traced by natural cubic splines

The Orphan–Chenab stream is tidal debris from a likely dwarf galaxy, traced over about 200° of sky. Koposov, Erkal, Li et al. (2023) combined Gaia, S⁵ and other spectroscopic surveys to fit the stream’s position and motion along the stream angle φ₁. Those measurements constrained the masses of the Milky Way and the Large Magellanic Cloud.

Every curve here is computed in your browser from the knot values the authors released. Move along a panel, or use the φ₁ slider, to pick a point on the stream. Drag the sky to turn it; scroll or pinch to zoom. From the keyboard, focus the sky and use the arrow keys to pan, or Shift + arrow keys to turn it.

All three panels share the φ₁ axis in degrees. Dots are the released knot values. Bars run from the 16th to the 84th posterior percentile at each knot. The curves evaluate the natural splines through the knots. The distance plot converts the modulus spline to kpc and shades the RR Lyrae gap. Each curve ends at its own first and last knot, because the spline is never extrapolated.

This is what the spline solver computes: the curvature z = S″ at each v_gsr knot. At the two ringed ends z is held at 0, which is what makes the spline natural.

How the spline works

With knots ni, values vi and spacings hi = ni+1 − ni, the interior second derivatives solve a tridiagonal system hi−1zi−1 + 2(hi−1+hi)zi + hizi+1 = 6(bi − bi−1), where bi = (vi+1−vi)/hi. The end values are fixed at z = 0. On [ni, ni+1] the curve is S(x) = zi+1(x−ni)³/6h + zi(ni+1−x)³/6h + (vi+1/h − hzi+1/6)(x−ni) + (vi/h − hzi/6)(ni+1−x). Stan solves the system with a dense solve. Here it is solved with the Thomas algorithm, which gives the same solution. In the paper the knot values are fitted in Stan using binned Gaia star counts for the sky track and individual stars for distance and velocity.

From the track to the masses

The curve shows the present-day centreline. After correction for the Sun’s motion, the study’s proper motions show southern stars moving across the track. The two proper-motion components are omitted here. The masses come from a separate model fitted to the full 6-D track. It simulates tidal stripping from a progenitor and includes the Milky Way’s motion in response to the LMC. The paper reports these enclosed masses for its preferred oblate Milky Way halo model.
